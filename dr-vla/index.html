The research

Abstract

Quadruped robots navigating shared outdoor spaces must understand complex scenes, anticipate change, and act in socially compliant ways. Egocentric web videos contain abundant human navigation experience, yet transferring it to robots is difficult because humans and robots observe and move through the world differently.

We introduce DR-VLA, a Mixture-of-Transformers framework that transfers this experience through a domain-invariant scene representation (DISR). An understanding expert reasons about the scene, a world expert predicts its future structure, and a flow-matching action expert generates robot trajectories. Using EgoLynx, we first learn from 10,000 hours of human videos without action labels, then adapt to 2,000 teleoperated robot training clips. On the held-out robot test split, DR-VLA improves the Quadruped Navigation Score by approximately 5% and reduces final heading error by 14.6%, each relative to the strongest competing result. A separately trained NAVSIM model reaches 92.1 PDMS without web-video pretraining.

01 / Motivation

Human navigation experience,
shared with robots.

People already demonstrate how to move through crowds, navigate around obstacles, and respond to changing scenes—in everyday videos.

An abundant source of experience

Robot demonstrations require teleoperation and safety oversight. Human egocentric videos provide a much broader range of streets, weather, and social interactions, offering a scalable way to learn navigation priors.

Two gaps to bridge

A human’s head-mounted camera differs from a robot’s low-mounted fisheye views. Human motion also lacks executable robot action labels. Transferring scene understanding and future prediction requires an interface that can span these differences.

Scene structure provides a shared interface:
what is present, where it is, and how it relates to its surroundings.

02 / Method

Understand. Anticipate. Act.

DR-VLA predicts the future in a domain invariant scene representation, then grounds that prediction in a robot trajectory.

Semantic structure

What is in the scene?

Segmentation provides a shared vocabulary of entities, including pedestrians and vehicles.

Spatial structure

Where is it?

Relative depth captures geometry while reducing scale differences caused by camera height.

Relational structure

How does it relate?

Causal spatiotemporal attention encodes relationships across entities, geometry, and time.

EXPERT 01

Understanding

A vision-language model interprets observations and the navigation instruction, learning scene reasoning through caption supervision.

EXPERT 02

World

Predicts future DISR latents to learn how scene structure evolves, using human videos without robot action labels.

EXPERT 03

Action

Uses understanding and world-expert features to generate executable trajectories through flow matching.

03 / EgoLynx

Learn at scale.
Adapt to the robot.

Complementary human and robot data support two stages of training under a shared annotation format.

Stage 1 · EgoLynx-Web
10,000 hours

Human videos, without action labels

Curated egocentric videos cover urban and rural environments, different times of day, and diverse weather. The understanding and world experts learn scene reasoning and future DISR prediction.

Stage 2 · EgoLynx-Robot
2,000 training clips

Grounding in real robot motion

Approximately 17 hours of teleoperated wheeled-legged robot navigation from real-world delivery settings. All three experts are fine-tuned, with robot action supervision and continued scene understanding and prediction.

Navigation instructionsScene captionsDISR targetsRobot actions · Stage 2

Robot evaluation uses a held-out test split from the same teleoperation collection, with no clips shared with the training split. Robot adaptation addresses the remaining viewpoint and embodiment differences.

05 / Qualitative results

Human priors meet robot grounding.

Predicted trajectories on replayed observations from four unseen scenes, comparing web-only, robot-only, and jointly trained variants.

Web-only

Steers around obstacles in these examples, but remains poorly calibrated to the robot’s metric scale. This ablation uses DPVO pseudo-trajectory supervision.

Robot-only

Predicts more suitably scaled motion, but follows socially compliant routes less consistently in the shown scenes.

Joint training

Combines human navigation priors with robot grounding. In the third scene, the predicted path avoids an obstacle and returns to the sidewalk.

Evaluation scope. The manuscript reports open-loop and pseudo-closed-loop evaluation with real-robot replay. Large-scale closed-loop deployment remains future work, and transfer still requires adaptation on robot data.
